#### Formula 1 Puts Austin on the World Stage While Driving Local Economic Opportunity

City of Austin
For Immediate Release

Release Date:
October 26, 2026

Contact:

Carlos Soto

Email

AUSTIN, TX — Following another Formula 1 United States Grand Prix weekend at Circuit of The Americas, Austin Economic Development is highlighting the broader impact a world-class international event can have across the local economy, from hotels and restaurants to entertainment venues, small businesses, hospitality workers and the city’s visitor economy.
Visit Austin projected that this year’s Formula 1 activity could generate more than $1 billion in economic impact, with roughly half attributed to direct spending, as thousands of race fans, teams, media representatives and other visitors spend time in the Austin area. Hotels were expected to reach approximately 92% to 95% occupancy during the event period .
While final economic results from the 2026 race will take time to measure, previous Formula 1 weekends illustrate how that activity moves through the local economy.
In its official post-event review of the 2021 U.S. Grand Prix, the Texas Governor’s Office estimated $331.4 million in primary direct spending in the Austin market. That included nearly $154.7 million in hotel and motel spending, $53.5 million at eating and drinking establishments, and approximately $58 million in shopping and entertainment spending. The state analysis also estimated approximately $30.6 million in incremental state tax receipts associated with that year's event.
Those impacts can be seen beyond economic models. During the 2022 Formula 1 weekend, CoStar reported that Austin’s upper-tier hotels averaged 91% occupancy, with an average daily room rate of $676 and approximately $29 million in room revenue over the three-day period. After the 2025 Grand Prix, Austin-Bergstrom International Airport screened 45,048 departing travelers on the Monday following the race, setting what was then a single-day passenger screening record for the airport.
“Economic development doesn’t always look like a ribbon cutting or a new office building. Sometimes, you can hear it coming around Turn 1,” said Mac Cummins, Director of Austin Economic Development. “People may come to Austin for the race, but while they’re here, they experience our restaurants, our music, our hospitality, our neighborhoods and the local businesses that give this city its personality. That’s where a major event becomes something bigger than a weekend.”
Formula 1 also gives Austin an international platform that few recurring events can match. Formula 1 reports a global fanbase of more than 830 million people, while its U.S. fanbase reached approximately 52 million in 2025. Recent Austin Grands Prix have drawn more than 400,000 cumulative race-weekend attendees, and Formula 1 says recent editions have surpassed 430,000. The U.S. Grand Prix has been extended at Circuit of The Americas through 2034, giving Austin a long-term presence on one of the world’s most visible international sports calendars.
The event is also increasingly a showcase for Austin’s entertainment and creative economy. The 2026 weekend featured headline performances by Maroon 5, Post Malone and Alesso, alongside Austin and Texas artists performing throughout the venue. Beyond the racetrack, the weekend creates another stage for live music, food and entertainment, connecting an international audience with the creative industries and cultural identity that are central to Austin’s economy and global reputation. Visit Austin describes the event as four days of racing, live music, culinary experiences and other entertainment.
That exposure feeds into a much larger local visitor economy. Visit Austin reports that the city welcomes roughly 30 million annual visitors, with visitor spending generating an estimated $11.1 billion in economic impact and the hospitality and tourism industry supporting approximately 148,000 local jobs.
Visitor spending also supports Austin’s tax base. The City receives revenue from sales, hotel stays, vehicle rentals and mixed-beverage sales. Some hotel tax revenue is reinvested in tourism, cultural arts and historic preservation, helping support the places and experiences that continue to make Austin a destination.
“The long-term opportunity is turning that attention into lasting value for the people who work these events, the businesses and cultural places visitors discover, and the broader community,” Cummins said. “We also know an event of this scale brings traffic, crowds and real disruption for residents, and we shouldn’t minimize that. Our responsibility is to manage that balance thoughtfully and make sure the opportunity extends beyond the racetrack. When people, place and business reinforce one another, that is economic development.”

Austin Economic Development sees major events such as Formula 1 as one component of a broader strategy to build a competitive and resilient economy. The City’s 2026 Economic Development Roadmap calls for Austin to intentionally strengthen its tax base, market the city’s combined economic and cultural strengths globally, expand international economic-development activity, support small and creative businesses and use destination-based development to strengthen Austin’s long-term competitiveness.
“Events like Formula 1 give us a chance to introduce Austin to people who may be experiencing our city for the first time,” Cummins said. “The win is not just that they came. The win is that they leave understanding a little more about who we are and wanting to come back.”
